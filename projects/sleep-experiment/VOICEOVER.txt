In the 1940s, Soviet scientists locked five prisoners in a room full of a gas that made sleep impossible.
Stay awake for thirty days, they were told, and you walk free.
For four days, everything was fine.
On day five, they started whispering into the microphones, betraying each other.
On day nine, one man screamed for three hours, until his voice tore.
The others didn't even look up.
Two of them tore pages out of their books, and glued them over the windows with their own filth.
Then the room went silent.
For three days, the microphones heard nothing, but the oxygen meter said five people were working very hard.
On day fourteen, the scientists warned them: we're coming in.
A calm voice answered: we don't want to come out anymore.
When the soldiers opened the door, they screamed.
One prisoner was dead, and the drain was clogged with what was left of him.
The others hadn't eaten in ten days, and they begged for one thing: turn the gas back on.
The commander ordered the room sealed again, with the scientists locked inside.
One scientist drew his pistol, shot the commander, and aimed at the last prisoner.
What are you? he asked.
It smiled. We're what you lock away every night, when you fall asleep.
So, sleep tight.
