# dna-drive: What if you used your DNA as storage?
Length target 55-70 s. Hypothetical "what if" framing; the what-if numbers are labelled as arithmetic.

## Facts and sources
- 215 PB per gram, read back perfectly (DNA Fountain, 2017): https://magazine.engineering.columbia.edu/spring-2018/dna-next-hard-drive , bioRxiv https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/074237v4
- Movie + operating system stored in DNA: https://datascience.columbia.edu/news/2017/researchers-store-computer-operating-system-and-short-movie-on-dna/
- 37.2 trillion cells: Bianconi et al. 2013, Annals of Human Biology 40(6):463-471, DOI 10.3109/03014460.2013.807878 (an estimate).
- Write cost about $3,500/MB (chemical synthesis): https://wyss.harvard.edu/technology/DNA-data-storage/ ; reading takes hours to days: https://blocksandfiles.com/2026/01/16/dna-data-storage-when-the-physics-work-but-the-economics-dont/
- Genome ~3 billion base pairs x 2 bits = ~750 MB: arithmetic; NHGRI: https://www.genome.gov/Pages/Education/Smithsonian_Exhibition/Human_Genome_Project_FAQ.pdf
- 28 ZB = 37.2e12 cells x 0.75 GB (one genome copy per cell): hypothetical arithmetic.
- DNA lifespan "thousands of years": optimistic end; sources range centuries to tens of thousands (what-if video).
- Copying errors at cell division: general biology (mutations arise in replication).
