## Facts and sources
| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ~750 MB of DNA per cell | Simplified: one genome copy (~3.1 billion base pairs x 2 bits ≈ 775 MB). Body cells carry two copies (~1.5 GB) | NHGRI genome FAQ https://www.genome.gov/Pages/Education/Smithsonian_Exhibition/Human_Genome_Project_FAQ.pdf |
| 37 trillion cells | Estimate (37.2 T). A later estimate puts it nearer 30 T, and most of those are red blood cells, which have no DNA | Bianconi et al. 2013, DOI 10.3109/03014460.2013.807878 ; Sender, Fuchs & Milo 2016, PLOS Biology DOI 10.1371/journal.pbio.1002533 |
| 28 zettabytes | What-if arithmetic: 37.2e12 x 0.75 GB ≈ 28 ZB | arithmetic |
| 2017: 215 PB per gram | **Flag:** 215 PB/g is the density the method can reach; the experiment itself stored about 2 MB | Erlich & Zielinski, Science 2017 (DNA Fountain) https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/074237v4 ; https://magazine.engineering.columbia.edu/spring-2018/dna-next-hard-drive |
| A paperclip ≈ 1 gram | True (standard paperclip ~1 g) | general reference |
| Movie + operating system stored and read back error-free | True (1895 Lumière film + KolibriOS) | https://datascience.columbia.edu/news/2017/researchers-store-computer-operating-system-and-short-movie-on-dna/ |
| DNA lasts thousands of years | True for cold, dry or encapsulated DNA; optimistic for DNA in the open | Grass et al. 2015, Angewandte Chemie DOI 10.1002/anie.201411378 |
| Writing costs thousands of $ per MB | Dated: ~$3,500/MB was the 2017 figure; it has dropped since, but is still far above any drive | https://wyss.harvard.edu/technology/DNA-data-storage/ ; https://blocksandfiles.com/2026/01/16/dna-data-storage-when-the-physics-work-but-the-economics-dont/ |
| Reading takes hours | True (sequencing takes hours to days) | same |
| Cells copy DNA at division; copies carry errors | True (replication errors are a source of mutations) | general biology |
| Labs keep DNA in cold, dry tubes | True | Grass et al. 2015 |
