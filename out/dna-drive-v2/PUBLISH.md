## Description
What if you could save your files inside your own body? You already do: it's called DNA. One cell holds about one old USB stick of data. In 2017, scientists stored a movie and an operating system in DNA and read both back perfectly. So why aren't we walking hard drives? Price, speed, and typos.

Fact check (sources):
| Claim | Verdict | Source |
|---|---|---|
| ~750 MB of DNA per cell | Simplified: one genome copy ≈ 750-775 MB; most body cells hold two copies (~1.5 GB) | NHGRI: https://www.genome.gov/Pages/Education/Smithsonian_Exhibition/Human_Genome_Project_FAQ.pdf |
| 37 trillion cells | Estimate (Bianconi 2013); newer estimate ~30 trillion, most of them red blood cells with no DNA | https://doi.org/10.3109/03014460.2013.807878 · https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pbio.1002533 |
| 28 zettabytes | What-if arithmetic (37 T x 750 MB) | — |
| 215 petabytes per gram (2017) | ⚠️ That's the density the method can reach; the experiment itself stored ~2 MB | https://www.biorxiv.org/content/10.1101/074237v4 |
| Movie + OS stored and read back error-free | True | https://datascience.columbia.edu/news/2017/researchers-store-computer-operating-system-and-short-movie-on-dna/ |
| DNA lasts thousands of years | True when kept cold and dry | https://doi.org/10.1002/anie.201411378 |
| Writing costs thousands of $ per MB, reading takes hours | ⚠️ ~$3,500/MB was the 2017 figure; it has dropped since, but is still far above any drive | https://wyss.harvard.edu/technology/DNA-data-storage/ · https://blocksandfiles.com/2026/01/16/dna-data-storage-when-the-physics-work-but-the-economics-dont/ |
| Copies carry errors at cell division | True | general biology |
